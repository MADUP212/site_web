Troops, Indo-Pacific vs Europe, exercises, 2014-2025

Marc-Antoine Dupuis and Adam Ménard, Université Laval

November 2026

The US defense budget in constant 2025 dollars, to compare years with each other. 2008: 1,020 billion. Click: the budget falls until 2015 (793 billion), with the end of the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan and the automatic cuts of 2013. Click: it then rises again under Trump 1 and Biden, up to 1,104 billion in 2025, the highest of the period; 968 billion in 2026. Caution: the IISS revised the 2026 figure downwards in a more recent export (6 November), which we do not use yet.

Six regional commands divide up the worldCommands

Each country falls under a US geographic combatant command

INDOPACOM: from India to the Pacific, China and Japan included

EUCOM: Europe, Turkiye and Russia

Measure: geographic combatant command of each country or territory in 2026, from the us_cocom codebook (Unified Command Plan). Light grey: not coded (Palestine, French Guiana, New Caledonia…). Base map: Natural Earth.

The six US geographic combatant commands (Unified Command Plan): NORTHCOM (North America), SOUTHCOM (Latin America and the Caribbean), EUCOM (Europe and Russia), AFRICOM (Africa, except Egypt), CENTCOM (Middle East and Central Asia, with Egypt and Pakistan) and INDOPACOM (South and East Asia, Oceania). The functional commands (cyber, space, transport…) have no territory. Click: INDOPACOM, the largest, from India to the Pacific. Click: EUCOM, Europe, Turkiye and Russia. Two countries changed command: Israel moved from EUCOM to CENTCOM in 2021, Greenland from EUCOM to NORTHCOM in 2025; the map shows the current situation. The commands also cover the oceans; in the following charts, each sea is assigned to a command (South China Sea to INDOPACOM, Mediterranean Sea and North Sea to EUCOM, Persian Gulf and Red Sea to CENTCOM, Caribbean Sea to SOUTHCOM).

Three regional commands, 98% of the troopsOn land

INDOPACOM and EUCOM: 180,300 troops in 2025, 82%

CENTCOM: from 60,700 to 35,400, with the exit from Afghanistan

AFRICOM and Americas: 5,400 troops in 2025

Measure: military personnel deployed in the countries and territories of each regional command, by year; seas and oceans excluded. Americas = NORTHCOM + SOUTHCOM. Dotted lines: change of president. IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

The six regional commands, on land only. Troop series stop in 2025: the IISS 2026 export is an exact copy of 2025. INDOPACOM and EUCOM together hold 82% of the troops in 2025: 94,800 and 85,500. Click: CENTCOM, the Middle East and Central Asia, goes from 60,700 troops in 2014 to 35,400 in 2025. Afghanistan explains most of it: 28,970 troops in 2014, 9,800 in 2015 after the end of the combat mission, 0 in 2021 after the withdrawal. The rise from 2017 to 2019 comes from Afghanistan (up to 16,500), Iraq (up to 9,000) and Syria (fight against the Islamic State). Kuwait (10,000), Qatar (9,000) and the United Arab Emirates (4,000) remain the main bases. With INDOPACOM and EUCOM: 98% of the troops every year. Click: the others weigh little: AFRICOM 4,100 (mostly Djibouti), NORTHCOM 250 and SOUTHCOM 1,000 (Guantánamo, Honduras), grouped here as "Americas".

At sea: from the Mediterranean to the CaribbeanAt sea

Europe: up to 11,500 troops at sea in 2024, none since 2025

Middle East: 11,100 in 2023, mostly Persian Gulf and Red Sea

2025: Caribbean Sea (11,000) and South China Sea (7,500)

Measure: US military personnel at sea, by sea and year (situation at year end), 2021-2025. No personnel counted in the Atlantic, Pacific or Indian Ocean since 2021. IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

Troops at sea, sea by sea; the IISS counts them only since 2021. Each circle is the presence at year end, not a continuous presence. Europe first: in the Mediterranean Sea from 2022 to 2024 (6,380 in 2022, of which 380 in NATO's Standing Maritime Group), and 7,000 in the North Sea in 2024; nothing since 2025. Click: the Middle East. 3,000 in the Persian Gulf in 2021; in 2023, 6,200 in the Gulf and 4,500 in the Red Sea (Houthi attacks); in 2024, 7,500 in the Arabian Sea. Click: in 2025, the troops at sea move to the Caribbean Sea (11,000, SOUTHCOM) and to the South China Sea (7,500, INDOPACOM). Each large presence matches a carrier strike group or an amphibious group listed by the IISS: Mediterranean 2022 and 2023, Gulf 2023, Arabian Sea and North Sea 2024, Caribbean and South China Sea 2025.

EUCOM, country by country. Germany remains the base: around 38,000 over the whole period, 45% of the command in 2025. Click: the change comes from the East. Exercises: Europe first, then the Indo-PacificExercises

EUCOM: ahead almost every year, 61 exercises in 2023

INDOPACOM: 60 exercises in 2025, almost twice Europe

The total ranges from 29 exercises (2020) to 148 (2024): read with caution

Measure: exercises by start year and command of the location (counted in each if there are two). Americas = NORTHCOM + SOUTHCOM; Others = Atlantic, Arctic, no location. 2026 excluded (incomplete). IISS Military Balance+, Exercises, 2026.

Exercises with the United States, by the command where they take place; an exercise counts in the year it starts. EUCOM is ahead almost every year (except 2014, and a tie in 2022), with a peak of 61 exercises in 2023. Click: INDOPACOM rises and moves ahead in 2025: 60 exercises against 32 in Europe. Click: beware of the total, which ranges from 29 exercises in 2020 (the COVID year) to 148 in 2024; part of these differences probably comes from IISS recording, not from actual activity. NORTHCOM is mostly exercises on US soil (81 of 94); 17 exercises take place in two commands and count in both. 2026 is excluded: the data stop in March.

In days: Europe first, then the Indo-PacificExercises

EUCOM: 5,693 exercise-days in 2014-2025, 42% of the total

INDOPACOM: 596 days in 2025, ahead of Europe (485)

The total ranges from 411 days (2020) to 2,292 (2024): read with caution

Measure: exercise-days by year and command of the location (duration of each exercise, split between calendar years). Americas = NORTHCOM + SOUTHCOM; Others = Atlantic, Arctic, no location. IISS Military Balance+, Exercises, 2026.

The same exercises as the previous slide, but in days: a 10-day exercise weighs 10 times a one-day exercise. The IISS does not give the number of personnel involved; duration is the only measure of size available. EUCOM totals 5,693 days over 2014-2025, 42% of the 13,691 days, with a peak of 935 days in 2023. Click: INDOPACOM moves ahead in 2025, 596 days against 485; its exercises are shorter that year (9.5 days at the median, against 12). It was also ahead in 2022 (178 against 162). Click: the total varies a lot, from 411 days in 2020 (COVID) to 2,292 in 2024; part of it comes from IISS recording. The Americas weigh mostly in 2016 (539 days), with long exercises on US soil. An exercise spanning two years is split between them; an exercise in two commands counts in both.

More than eight exercises in ten are between countriesExercises

NATO: 134 exercises out of 997, almost all in Europe (128)

Between countries: 840, of which 260 with a single partner

2025: 4 NATO exercises, against 19 in 2024

Measure: exercises by start year, by framework: NATO (organisation named by the IISS) or between countries (United States and at least one partner, outside NATO). Not shown: 23 exercises with no partner. 2026 excluded. IISS Military Balance+, Exercises, 2026.

The framework of each exercise, from the IISS Organisations column. NATO frames 134 exercises out of 997, one in seven; 128 take place in Europe, a third of EUCOM's exercises (128 of 390). Click: almost all the others are between countries, outside the NATO framework: 840 exercises (84%), of which 260 with a single partner and 580 with several. Many still bring together NATO members, but without the organisation (for example the United States and Poland). A few fall under other organisations: ADMM-Plus (13), Gulf Cooperation Council, United Nations. 23 exercises have no partner. Click: in 2025, the IISS lists only 4 NATO exercises, against 19 to 20 a year in 2023 and 2024, while the total remains high (102). To be confirmed: IISS recording can lag behind.

Partners: NATO allies firstExercises

United Kingdom: in 392 exercises out of 997, two in five

10 of the first 12 partners are NATO members

Outside NATO: Australia (184) and Japan (183)

Measure: number of exercises in which each country takes part with the United States, 2014-2025 (start year). The first 12 of the 184 partner countries. IISS Military Balance+, Exercises, 2026.

997 exercises with the United States from 2014 to 2025; 23 have no partner. An exercise counts for each country taking part: half have at least 4, and large multinational exercises up to 49. The United Kingdom is the first partner, in 392 exercises, two in five; then France (295), Germany (248) and Canada (237). Click: ten of the first twelve are NATO members. Click: the other two are Australia and Japan, almost always in exercises in the Indo-Pacific (145 of 184 and 155 of 183). Korea (Republic of) comes next, in 16th place (124).

With Canada: more and more in the Indo-PacificExercises

Europe: up to 18 exercises in 2023, 11 in 2025

Indo-Pacific: from 2 in 2014 to 12 in 2024 and 9 in 2025

Canada takes part in about one US exercise in four

Measure: exercises in which Canada takes part with the United States, by start year and command of the location. Americas = NORTHCOM + SOUTHCOM; Others = AFRICOM, CENTCOM, no location. IISS Military Balance+, Exercises, 2026.

Exercises with Canada, year by year. The total goes from 9 in 2014 to 35 in 2017 and in 2023, then 22 in 2025. Europe remains the first location: up to 18 exercises in 2023, 11 in 2025; none in 2022, a year with few exercises listed by the IISS. Click: the Indo-Pacific rises, from 2 exercises in 2014 to 11 and 12 in 2023 and 2024, then 9 in 2025, almost as many as in Europe. Click: Canada's share remains stable: it takes part in about one US exercise in four every year (20 to 30%), except in 2022 (14%). The total therefore mostly follows the number of exercises listed by the IISS. In North and Latin America, 0 to 9 a year. Only one exercise brings together the United States and Canada with no other country: Guerrier nordique 2023, in Nunavut. Caution: the IISS database contains no NORAD exercise (Vigilant Shield, Amalgam Dart), although they are binational; the bilateral relationship is therefore underestimated. Further research to do.
