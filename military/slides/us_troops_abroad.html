Defense budget and deployments, 2008-2026

Marc-Antoine Dupuis and Adam Ménard, Université Laval

November 2026

The defense budget weighs less and less

US defense budget, as a share of GDP

2008: 4.7% of GDP

2026: 3.1%, a third less

Measure: defense budget as a % of GDP, by year. Dotted lines: change of president. IISS Military Balance+, Economics, 2026.

The defense budget falls from 4.7% of GDP in 2008 to 3.1% in 2026. Most of the decline happens under Obama (2010 to 2015); since then, it stays between 3.2% and 3.6%, whoever the president. In constant dollars, however, the budget is back to its 2008 level: the economy simply grew faster.

Around 220,000 troops abroad

US military personnel deployed abroad

Since 2021, the IISS also counts sailors at sea

Excluding sea: 219,000 in 2014, 221,000 in 2026

Measure: military personnel deployed abroad, by year. Excluding sea: without seas and oceans. The IISS data for 2025 and 2026 are identical. IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

Total US military personnel deployed abroad: from 219,000 in 2014 to 240,000 in 2026, with a peak of 252,000 in 2023. Click: part of the increase is a change in how the data are recorded: before 2021, the IISS lists ships without personnel; since then, it counts sailors at sea (about 20,000 in 2026). Click: excluding sea, the level is almost the same in 2014 and 2026, around 220,000. The 2025 and 2026 data are identical in the IISS export.

Half of the troops in three countries

Japan, Germany, Korea (Republic of): 51% of troops in 2026

Far behind: Kuwait and Italy, 4 to 5% each

Measure: each country's share of military personnel deployed abroad, in %. The five countries with the highest average share, 2014-2026. IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

Three allied countries host half of US military personnel abroad: Japan (23%), Germany (16%) and Korea (Republic of) (12%), i.e. 51% in 2026, against 54% in 2014. These shares change little from one president to the next. Click: the next two, Kuwait and Italy, account for 4 to 5% each.

Asia loses weight, Europe gains

Asia: 52% of troops in 2014, 39% in 2026

Europe: from 31% to 36%, mostly after 2022

Middle East and North Africa: 15% today, as in 2014

Maritime: 8%, but counted only since 2021

Measure: each region's share of military personnel deployed abroad, in %. Not shown: Sub-Saharan Africa (2%) and other regions (less than 1%). IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

Share of each region. Asia remains the first region, but falls from 52% to 39%. Click: Europe rises from 31% to 36%, with a jump in 2022 (invasion of Ukraine). Click: the Middle East and North Africa rises to 21% in 2017, then returns to 15%. Click: troops at sea (Maritime) account for 8%, but the IISS counts them only since 2021: part of Asia's decline comes from this change in recording.

Overview: out of Afghanistan, reinforcing the East

Afghanistan: −28,970 troops since 2014

Poland: +10,000, mostly since 2022

Elsewhere, changes of 3,000 to 6,000

Measure: change in the number of military personnel since 2014, by year; the six destinations that changed most, seas excluded. Jordan: present in 2014 with no personnel figure, change computed from 0. IISS Military Balance+, Deployments, 2026.

Change since 2014. The largest change: Afghanistan, almost 29,000 fewer troops, with the 2021 withdrawal under Biden (the Doha agreement was signed under Trump 1). Click: the largest increase, Poland, +10,000, mostly since 2022. Click: elsewhere, Japan (+5,750), Romania (+4,000), Jordan (+3,000) and Kuwait (−3,000).
